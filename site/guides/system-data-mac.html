What is System Data on a Mac, and how do you clear it?

By the Gigapolis team · Updated 4 November 2026

System Data (called “Other” in older versions of macOS) is the catch-all for everything macOS doesn't sort into a category like Apps, Photos or Documents. On most Macs, the big parts are local Time Machine snapshots, caches and logs. Those can be cleared safely. The rest is macOS itself, which you should leave alone.

What's in System Data?

Apple doesn't publish an exact list, but System Data usually includes:

  • Local Time Machine snapshots: copies of changed files that Time Machine keeps on your Mac between backups.
  • Caches: files apps and macOS keep so things load faster.
  • Logs and diagnostic reports.
  • Virtual memory (swap files) and the sleep image.
  • iPhone and iPad backups made in Finder.
  • Files in hidden or system folders that macOS can't put in another category, such as virtual machines, app support files and some developer tools.
  • macOS itself: the system volume and its updates.

Why is System Data so big?

The usual cause is local snapshots. If Time Machine can't reach its backup drive for a while, it keeps snapshots on your Mac, and they can grow to tens of gigabytes. macOS is meant to remove them automatically when space runs low, but the Storage bar still counts them, which is why System Data can look alarming. Large caches (from browsers, video apps, Xcode or Docker) are the next most common cause.

Remove local Time Machine snapshots

Open Terminal (in Applications → Utilities) and list the snapshots:

tmutil listlocalsnapshots /

Each line ends in a date like 2026-11-01-093015. To delete one, use that date:

sudo tmutil deletelocalsnapshots 2026-11-01-093015

You'll be asked for your Mac's password. This only removes the copies on your Mac. Backups on your Time Machine drive aren't touched. Connecting your backup drive and letting a backup finish also lets macOS clear old snapshots on its own.

Clear caches and logs

In Finder, press ⇧⌘G and go to ~/Library/Caches. Each folder belongs to an app. Quit the app, then move its folder's contents to the Trash. Logs live in ~/Library/Logs. Restart your Mac afterwards. Our guide to clearing cache on a Mac has more detail, including browsers.

Other things hiding in System Data

  • iPhone and iPad backups. In Finder, select your device and click Manage Backups to delete old ones.
  • Developer files. Xcode's DerivedData, simulators and Docker images can take tens of gigabytes.
  • Video editing caches. Final Cut, Premiere and DaVinci Resolve keep render files that can be rebuilt.
  • Mail attachments saved in the Mail library.

What not to delete

Don't delete files in /System, /Library or /private by hand, and don't turn off System Integrity Protection to make room. macOS needs a certain amount of System Data to run, and on a modern Mac 12–20 GB of it is normal.

Questions

Is it safe to delete System Data on a Mac?

Parts of it. Local Time Machine snapshots, caches and logs are safe to remove. Core macOS files are not, and macOS stops you deleting them anyway.

Why does System Data keep growing?

Usually because Time Machine keeps local snapshots while your backup drive is disconnected, and because app caches grow over time. Connecting your backup drive and clearing large caches normally brings it down.

What is a normal size for System Data?

There's no fixed number, but 12–20 GB is common on a modern Mac. Anything much over 50 GB usually means snapshots or a large cache.
